Xbox Launches XP Division for Film, TV, and Live Events

This post may include affiliate links. We may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Xbox is thinking well beyond the controller. The company has officially launched a new internal division called XP, purpose-built to expand the Xbox brand into film, television, live events, consumer products, and creator partnerships — everything that isn't a video game, but is unmistakably Xbox.

What XP Actually Is

This isn't a pivot away from games. It's an acknowledgment that Xbox — as a franchise, a cultural identity, and an IP portfolio — has outgrown its hardware roots. XP formalizes work that was already happening across scattered teams inside Microsoft's gaming division, pulling it all under one centralized structure with clear investment priorities.

Think of it as Xbox building the infrastructure other entertainment giants already have. Disney has its parks and merchandise machine. Netflix has live events and physical experiences. Xbox, sitting on decades of beloved IP from Halo to Forza to Minecraft, has long had the raw material. XP is the apparatus to actually monetize and expand it.

Why This Matters Right Now

The timing is deliberate. Game-to-screen adaptations are having a genuine cultural moment — and Xbox has watched competitors capitalize while its own IP sat largely untapped on the big and small screen. With XP, the company is signaling it wants a seat at that table, with the organizational muscle to back it up.

Live events and brand partnerships are the other half of the equation. These are high-margin, high-visibility plays that deepen fan relationships outside of release cycles. For Xbox, which has struggled to match PlayStation's cultural cachet in recent years, XP is as much a brand-building exercise as it is a business one.

Who Should Pay Attention

  • Xbox fans: Expect more official live experiences, licensed products, and eventually screen adaptations of major franchises.
  • Brand and creator partners: XP creates a cleaner entry point for collaborations — one team, one conversation.
  • Industry watchers: This is Microsoft treating Xbox like a full entertainment brand, not just a gaming platform. That's a strategic shift worth tracking.

XP doesn't guarantee blockbuster films or sold-out arenas overnight. But it does mean Xbox is finally building the machine to make those things possible — and that the next chapter of the brand will likely look very different from the last one.
