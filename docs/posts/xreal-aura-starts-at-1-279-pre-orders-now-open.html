Xreal Aura Starts at $1,279 — Pre-Orders Now Open

This post may include affiliate links. We may earn a commission at no extra cost to you.

Xreal has officially opened pre-orders for the Xreal Aura — the transparent XR glasses developed in partnership with Google — starting at $1,279. The announcement marks the first time consumers can put money down on one of the most talked-about devices in the augmented reality space.

What Is the Xreal Aura?

The Aura is the product of Google's re-entry into the XR hardware market, this time built in collaboration with Xreal rather than developed fully in-house. The glasses are designed to be lightweight — weighing in at around 100 grams — and use transparent lenses, distinguishing them from opaque VR headsets that fully block out the real world. The goal is a device you might actually wear outside a demo room.

Google's software and AI layer is expected to be central to the experience, with Xreal handling much of the optics and industrial design. The pairing makes sense on paper: Xreal has years of consumer AR hardware experience; Google brings platform scale and developer relationships.

A Crowded Market at a Premium Price

The $1,279 starting price positions the Aura firmly in premium territory — well above impulse-buy range, but below the ceiling set by enterprise-grade headsets. According to reporting by The Verge, the Aura is entering a competitive landscape that includes Meta's own lightweight glasses push, with both products drawing attention from early hands-on journalists in the San Francisco Bay Area earlier this month.

That comparison matters for buyers trying to decide where to commit. The Aura's transparent display approach is a fundamentally different design philosophy than fully immersive headsets — it's built for people who want ambient computing layered over reality, not a replacement for it.

Who Should Pre-Order?

  • Early AR adopters who've been waiting for a Google-backed product with real display transparency.
  • Developers already invested in Google's ecosystem who want first-mover access to the platform.
  • XR enthusiasts who tried Xreal's previous hardware and want to see what a Google partnership unlocks.

If you're a casual buyer or still on the fence about AR wearables as a category, $1,279 is a steep bet on hardware that hasn't yet proven itself in daily use. But for anyone tracking this space closely, the Aura is now a real, orderable product — and that alone changes the conversation.

Pre-orders are live now. Full details via The Verge.
