Screen Tearing: Why It Happens and How to Fix It

If you've ever seen a horizontal split cutting across your game mid-frame, you've met screen tearing. It's distracting, it's ugly, and it's one of those problems that once you notice, you can't unsee. The good news: it's fixable, and usually without a hardware upgrade.

What Actually Causes Screen Tearing

Tearing happens when your GPU and your monitor fall out of sync. Your GPU is pushing out frames as fast as it can — sometimes far faster than your display can show them. When a new frame starts rendering before the monitor finishes drawing the previous one, you get two frames displayed at once, sliced across the screen. The result is that jagged horizontal break.

It's most visible in fast-panning scenes and competitive shooters, where frame rates spike and camera movement is aggressive. High-end GPUs can actually make it worse — the faster your card, the more likely it outruns your display.

Your Fix Options, Ranked by Setup

  • V-Sync: The classic solution. Forces your GPU to match your monitor's refresh rate. It kills tearing but introduces input lag, which is a real tradeoff in fast-paced games.
  • G-Sync (NVIDIA) / FreeSync (AMD): The modern answer. These adaptive sync technologies flip the equation — instead of locking the GPU, they let the monitor dynamically match the GPU's output. No tearing, minimal lag. You need a compatible monitor and GPU, but if you're building or upgrading, this is the standard worth targeting.
  • Fast Sync / Enhanced Sync: Manufacturer-specific options that try to split the difference — reducing tearing without the full input lag penalty of V-Sync. Worth testing if you're already in the NVIDIA or AMD ecosystem.
  • Frame rate caps: Capping your frame rate just below your monitor's refresh rate reduces the conditions that cause tearing in the first place. A simple, underrated fix that works even on older hardware.

Which Fix Is Right for You

Competitive players who live and die by response time should look at adaptive sync first — G-Sync and FreeSync have matured to the point where the monitor selection is wide and prices are reasonable. Casual gamers on older setups will find that enabling V-Sync in-game or capping frame rates solves most issues without spending anything.

The driver-level tools from both NVIDIA and AMD let you apply these settings globally or per-game, so you don't have to choose one approach for everything. Start there before assuming you need new hardware.

Screen tearing is a solvable problem. The fix just depends on knowing what's actually creating the mismatch in your setup.
